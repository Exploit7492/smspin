FAQ

Common questions answered

Is it legal to use an SMS housekeeper?+

Yes, using a temporary number is legal for privacy and testing. Make sure you're not breaking the Terms of Service of the platform you're signing up for. SMSPin is not affiliated with any app or website. Please follow each app's terms and local regulations.

Why isn't my code arriving?+

A few things can go wrong: the app might have flagged the number (due to historical abuse on the other blocklist), the locale could be congested, or the provider's server may be temporarily overwhelmed. Our system allows number release and an automatic refund if no code arrives.

Difference between a one-time (pay) and rent (long-term)?+

A one-time payment is for short-term use; rent a number for days or weeks when you need repeated OTPs or a longer verification window (like connecting WhatsApp). Costs are fixed per long usage.

What should you never use a temp number for?+

Never use them for emergency 2FA on financial accounts, healthcare, or anything you need to attribute personally. Banking and social human verification belong on your personal device.

Can I automate many registrations at once, or will I get blocked?+

Automating hundreds of signups on the same service can trigger anti-fraud flags, even if they use new numbers. Automation is best for varied, legitimate tests. SMSPin provides a rotating system, but your IP/mac address must align.

What Is an SMS Verification Housekeeper?

An SMS verification housekeeper is, essentially, a service or workflow that systematically manages the messy job of receiving and organizing OTP codes for all your digital accounts. Instead of putting your personal number on every signup, trial, or service check, a housekeeper steps in to receive those codes on dedicated, disposable virtual numbers.

You grab the code, use it, and move on without exposing your real number:

  • The term covers both the process and the service, essentially categorizing virtual numbers for transactional SMS verification.

  • It's less about "checking messages" and more about centralizing OTP reception in real time, all in one place.

  • Unlike a simple "second number," a housekeeper centralizes every incoming verification request in a dashboard, manually or automatically.

  • Apps like WhatsApp and Telegram don't know your actual device, only the number they received their code to.

The concept itself is simple: swap the physical SIM for a virtual number that can be active for a few minutes or up to 30 days. That keeps your personal SIM from ever becoming the default "main key" for everything digital.

In plain words: the SMS verification housekeeper is a smart way to keep your primary SIM private for important things (banking, personal accounts), while every other trial and registration runs through a dedicated service. That's the straightforward meaning.

SMS Verification Housekeeper Webhook Setup: Real-Time Code Delivery

Here's where the housekeeper becomes truly hands-free: webhooks turn it into an event-driven stream.

Instead of manually polling the API, the server sends an HTTP POST to your endpoint as soon as a verification code arrives. The housekeeper pushes updates to you, so your app never misses a beat, and you avoid cluttering your codebase with unnecessary poll loops.

How the webhook works:

  • Event types: You get notified about things like "code received" and expiry warnings.

  • Payload: A simple JSON body containing the number and the code, easy to parse.

  • Endpoints: You provide a URL your server handles; SMSPin calls it when events fire.

Setup process:

  1. Go to the API dashboard and add your webhook URL.

  2. Choose which events to subscribe to ("code received" is the key one).

  3. Send a test payload to verify everything's configured correctly.

This is perfect for CI/CD environments where your environment can't manually grab data. No more scraping SMS status every 10 seconds; the system pushes events in real time, reducing load on your server.
