Why Novo Requires SMS Verification in the First Place

Novo uses SMS verification to confirm you're a real person, not a bot, and to link a location to your account. Since Novo is a business banking platform, they're strict about who signs up, which means their SMS checks are more aggressive than a typical social media app.

  • Novo (like many US fintechs) relies on SMS to comply with KYC (Know Your Customer) regulations, not just to confirm a login.

  • You'll hit OTP prompts when creating an account, logging in from a new device, or adding a new recipient for payments.

  • The type of verification needed matters: a simple two-factor 2FA code is lower risk than an initial account setup sign-up.

If you're testing the sign-up flow or want a privacy buffer, a dedicated SMS verification service can handle these prompts without exposing your real number.

The Problem with SMS Verification: Novo Code Not Sent Errors

If you see "code not sent" or the code never arrives, it's usually because Novo's system already flagged the phone number as risky or unsupported. The bank's verification vendor automatically blocks VoIP and many prepaid numbers, so you get a silent failure instead of a text message.

  • Service providers like Twilio or TollFreeForwarding often block these numbers or don't work here because of Novo's carrier filters.

  • "Silent accept" mistakes happen: you see the confirmation box, but the text never arrives. A real SMS platform (like SMSPin) issues a real SIM-based number that appears in a normal mobile carrier range.

  • The error message is usually generic, so you have to troubleshoot systematically.

Novo's verification vendor uses carrier-level filtering. If your number isn't from a recognized mobile carrier, the OTP never even gets sent.

How to Fix Novo OTP Error and Verification Failed Messages

If you get a Novo OTP error or a "verification failed" message, check your number format (use the country code, no leading zero), try WiFi vs mobile data, and, most importantly, use a number that hasn't been used recently. The fix is rarely on your side; it's usually about the number source being low quality.

Troubleshooting steps:

  1. Check the number format. Enter the number in full international format: +1 followed by the 10-digit US number, no leading zero, no dashes.

  2. Avoid VoIP area codes. Novo's verifier blocks VoIP numbers by default. If you're using a free texting app number, that's your problem.

  3. Wait before resending. Wait the full 5–6 minutes before requesting a resend. Multiple rapid resends can auto-block the session.

  4. Switch networks. Sometimes mobile data routes OTPs faster than WiFi. Toggle airplane mode for 10 seconds and try again.

  5. Use a fresh, real number. If your free trial keeps failing, use a real-time rental number; it gives you a fresh, carrier-native number that passes Novo's filters.

If you've tried all of these and still see "verification failed," the number is the problem. Get a new one.
