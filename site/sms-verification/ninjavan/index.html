Is it legal to use a temporary number for Ninja Van verification?+

Yes, as long as you’re not using it to violate Ninja Van’s terms of service. Temporary numbers are a privacy tool; they’re legal for creating accounts for personal or business use. Why does my Ninja Van OTP keep saying “invalid”?+

The most common reason is that the code expired because you waited longer than 60–90 seconds to enter it. Other causes include a carrier delay that pushes delivery past the expiration window, requesting too many codes too quickly and locking the session, or using a virtual number that Ninja Van’s SMS gateway silently rejects.

Can I use the same temporary number for multiple Ninja Van accounts?+

No. Ninja Van permanently binds each phone number to one account during the initial OTP handshake. Attempting to reuse it, even after deleting the original account, will reliably produce a “phone not eligible” error. You must get a fresh, unused number for each separate login.

What’s the difference between a one-time number and a rental number for Ninja Van?+

A one-time number works for a single, immediate OTP and then disappears, which is perfect for initial signup only. A rental number stays active and assigned to you for a chosen period (24 hours, a week, or a month), making it suitable for later profile confirmations, password resets, or Ninja Van’s occasional security re-verification requests.

Why did my Ninja Van account get blocked even after successful SMS verification?+

Post-verification blocks often occur when Ninja Van’s risk system retroactively flags your account activity or the number's temporary pattern. This can also happen if multiple accounts were created in quick succession from the same IP address. Using a rented number for the first verification provides a more stable footprint during initial screening.

What should I NOT use a temporary number for on Ninja Van?+

You should never use a temporary number to commit fraud, impersonate another person or business, avoid a suspension imposed for violating courier terms, or engage in any activity that breaks Ninja Van’s delivery service agreement. Legitimate use cases include privacy protection, legitimate multi-account business management, and application testing.

How do I fix a “phone number not eligible” error on Ninja Van?+

Stop using your current number; it is permanently flagged, and no amount of waiting or retrying will clear it. Obtain a completely fresh, SIM-based temporary number that has never interacted with Ninja Van before. This single change resolves the “not eligible” block in virtually all cases.

You’re not doing anything sketchy; you need to manage a shipment, test an integration, maybe set up a second storefront. And then, boom. A red error message staring back at you like you’ve committed some unforgivable sin.

Why Ninja Van’s SMS Verification Fails (And What “Phone Not Eligible” Actually Means)

Ninja Van blocks numbers that have already verified other accounts, numbers tied to prepaid SIMs their system has flagged, or numbers from carriers they’ve quietly blocked. “Phone not eligible” is just the platform’s polite way of saying your number has hit a usage cap or comes from a source it doesn’t trust.

Think of it as a permanent record, not a temporary hiccup. Once a phone number completes a Ninja Van OTP handshake, it’s burned into their verification database for good. Try using that same number for another account, and you’ll be rejected instantly every time. This extends beyond individual numbers, too. Ninja Van’s fraud detection maintains lists of trusted and untrusted carriers, and VoIP numbers plus certain prepaid virtual operators often end up on the wrong side of that list. For broader context on how carriers share threat intelligence, GSMA's guidelines on carrier blocklist policies offer a useful look at why some numbers get blocked before they ever reach the app.

Even a recycled SIM card one that belonged to someone else before- can carry negative history that predates your ownership entirely. You’d never know it, but Ninja Van’s system does.

So when you see “phone not eligible,” it’s not asking you to wait or try again. It’s telling you that number is done. You need a completely fresh, unused number for that specific app.

The Real Reason You’re Seeing “OTP Invalid” or “Unknown Error” During Signup

“OTP invalid” usually means one of two things: the code expired before you entered it, or your network delayed delivery past the timeout window. “Unknown error” is murkier; it often points to a carrier-side rejection where Ninja Van’s SMS gateway couldn’t confirm delivery and just gave up without telling you why.

Most Ninja Van one-time passcodes have a brutally short expiration window, often somewhere between 60 and 120 seconds. That’s not much time if you’re flipping between your SMS app and the signup form, copying a code, and hoping your thumbs cooperate. The “unknown error” variant is even more frustrating because it’s usually not your fault at all. Ninja Van’s gateway tries to route a message to your number, doesn’t recognize the carrier as legitimate, and silently drops the attempt.

  • If you’re using a temporary number, make sure the provider routes SMS in real time. A 30-second delay in the dashboard can easily trigger the “invalid” flag.

  • Don’t hammer the “resend code” button. Requesting an OTP two or three times within a minute can lock your verification session for up to 24 hours. Wait a full two minutes between attempts.

  • Virtual number providers that rely on cloud-based numbers rather than physical SIM cards are the most common source of the “unknown” rejection banner. Avoid them for Ninja Van.

Ninja Van demands a different phone number for every single account. This isn’t a suggestion or a soft guideline; it’s a hard rule enforced at the OTP stage. Try the same number twice, and you’ll get slapped with “phone not eligible” immediately. What to Do If Your Ninja Van Account Is Blocked After Verification

If your Ninja Van account gets blocked after a successful verification, it’s usually because the system retroactively flagged the number as temporary or spotted unusual signup patterns from your IP address. Your best move is to contact Ninja Van’s support directly; they may ask for proof of identity or request a different phone number. The FTC offers useful context on how companies use account fraud detection guidelines that sometimes sweep up legitimate users.

A block doesn’t automatically mean your number failed. It can mean Ninja Van’s post-registration risk assessment put your account on hold. This happens more often when multiple accounts are created in rapid succession from a single connection.

  • If you used a one-time number, you won’t be able to prove ownership of it later. Renting a number for 24 hours gives you a window where the number remains yours, which is a much stronger position if you need to appeal a block.

  • Don’t rush to create another account immediately after a block. Wait at least 48 hours, and consider switching networks or using a different device to avoid IP-based bans.

  • Ninja Van’s support team handles the unblocking process entirely. SMSPin can confirm on your behalf that a code was delivered, but the decision to reinstate the account is theirs alone.

This is baked into the core of Ninja Van’s identity layer as an anti-abuse measure. Even if you fully delete the first account and wait six months, the number remains blocked. That makes a temporary number service the only scalable approach for anyone who legitimately needs multiple accounts, whether you’re testing new shipping workflows or managing separate business entities that each require their own courier dashboard.

Why SMS Verification Errors Happen on Ninja Van And How to Avoid Them

SMS errors on Ninja Van boil down to four main culprits: expired OTPs, carrier blocklisting, number reuse, and network timing mismatches. Avoiding most errors comes down to speed and number quality. A fresh number from a provider that uses real SIMs and a direct SMS route solves most cases instantly.

Delays are your worst enemy here. Ninja Van’s SMS gateway expects near-instant delivery, and messaging infrastructure providers like Twilio offer guidance on SMS gateway routing best practices that show how timeouts can cause automated security failures. If your code takes longer than 90 seconds to arrive, the session on Ninja Van’s side has often already expired, making the code technically “invalid” by the time you submit it. Inconsistent carrier routes only make this worse.

  • Choose a provider that explicitly uses physical SIMs, not cloud-based numbers, for Ninja Van verifications.

  • Stick to numbers from countries where Ninja Van actively operates to avoid geo-flagging issues.

  • If you hit any error, clear your browser cache or the Ninja Van app’s stored data before retrying. Session corruption from the failed attempt can produce false errors on a retry. A clean verification workflow always starts with a fresh number and a clean session.

Quick Fixes for Ninja Van Profile Phone Confirmation Errors

If you’re hitting errors while confirming your phone in the Ninja Van profile settings, first double-check that you’re typing the same number you registered with. A number mismatch is the most common cause of profile-level rejections.

Phone confirmation requests often fail for users who initially signed up with a one-time number. That number isn’t just unattended; it no longer exists. The platform sends a code into the void and then shows you an error, leaving you stuck in a loop.

  • Switch to a rental: The long-term fix is to switch to a rented number for the initial verification so the number is still active when you need to confirm it later in your profile settings.

  • Kill the VPN: If you’re running a VPN, turn it off during the profile confirmation. Ninja Van can and does block requests that appear to originate from anonymized IPs tied to VPN exits.

  • Check your clock: An unusual but real issue is a time zone mismatch. If your phone’s clock is significantly off from Ninja Van’s server time, the OTP’s generated timestamp can appear to be from the future or too far in the past, causing the cryptographic check to fail.

  • If you need a number to verify any profile quickly without cost, some services offer free/public testing numbers. Still, for reliability and speed, a dedicated private number is the safer bet.
