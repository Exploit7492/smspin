Why OfferUp Requires SMS Phone Verification 

OfferUp requires SMS verification to confirm you're a real person and reduce scam listings, a genuine problem on peer-to-peer marketplaces where buyers and sellers meet in person. The check adds trust between parties, but it also creates a massive hurdle when the code doesn't arrive, or the system rejects your number outright.

Most verification failures come down to one of three things: carrier filtering, app glitches, or using a phone number that's already flagged in OfferUp's anti-fraud database. It's rarely something you did wrong; it's technical friction between your carrier, OfferUp's SMS gateway, and the number itself.

Here's what's happening behind the scenes:

  • OfferUp ties your account reputation to a verified phone number, so unverified accounts carry lower trust signals with buyers.

  • The app uses third-party SMS gateways that can be rate-limited or blocked by certain carriers, causing delays.

  • If you've used the same number for multiple OfferUp accounts, it may be permanently flagged for re-verification.

  • Some VoIP numbers and prepaid SIMs are auto-rejected because they fail OfferUp's carrier lookup.

  • Verification protects the community, but it's also a data-mining moment. OfferUp captures your number and links it to your activity, browsing history, and listings.

The takeaway: the verification requirement is about safety, but the failure is almost always technical and fixable.

How to Verify Your Phone Number on OfferUp: Step-by-Step

Opening the OfferUp app and tapping "Verify Phone Number" in Settings is the simplest way to trigger the SMS check. OfferUp also prompts you automatically during signup and before you can post a listing, so you can't skip it if you're planning to sell.

The process sends a six-digit code via SMS, and you enter it into the app's text field to activate your account. If the code field never appears, force-close the app and retry on a stable Wi-Fi or cellular connection.

On iOS:

  1. Open the OfferUp app and log in.

  2. Tap Profile (bottom right) → Settings (gear icon) → Account.

  3. Tap Verify Phone Number.

  4. Enter your number in international format (e.g., +1 for US numbers).

  5. Wait for the SMS, enter the six-digit code, and tap Submit.

On Android:

  1. Open the OfferUp app and log in.

  2. Tap the hamburger menu (three lines, top left) → Settings → Account.

  3. Tap Phone Verification (the label may differ slightly from iOS).

  4. Enter your number with the correct country code.

  5. Enter the code when it arrives.

Pro tips:

  • Wait up to 60 seconds before requesting a resend; spamming the resend button triggers a cooldown.

  • If your phone auto-intercepts the code, copy it from the notification bar rather than opening your messages app.

  • After verification, your number is locked to the account and can't be swapped for 30 days; choose carefully.

OfferUp Signup Verification: What to Do When the Code Doesn't Arrive

The most common signup verification failure is a code that simply never lands in your SMS inbox. The usual culprits are carrier spam filtering, delayed gateway routing, or a typo in your phone number. Before you panic, check your spam or blocked-message folders and confirm the number you entered matches your SIM exactly; it's embarrassingly easy to fat-finger a digit.

If you've waited five minutes and still have nothing, request a resend once, then restart your phone to clear any network hang-ups. If you can't get the code even after multiple retries, a temporary number with a clean reputation is the fastest workaround, as long as it passes OfferUp's carrier checks.

Here's your troubleshooting checklist:

  • Check carrier filtering: Carriers like Verizon and T-Mobile aggressively filter short-code messages. Look for "Scam Block" or similar settings in your carrier app and disable them temporarily.

  • Check MVNO routing: OfferUp's gateway sends from a five-digit short code, which some MVNOs (Mint, Tello, etc.) route slower than standard numbers.

  • Wait 60–90 seconds before resending: Repeated requests can flag your session as spam and lock the number.

  • Turn off Wi-Fi: Some routers block short-code SMS delivery entirely; switch to cellular data.

  • Check dual-SIM setups: If your phone has two SIMs, confirm the second SIM isn't intercepting SMS delivery on the primary line.

OfferUp SMS Verification Error: Why You See "Verification Failed, Try Again"

The "Verification Failed, Try Again" error on OfferUp is a generic catch-all that appears when the app's server can't match your code, your number is flagged, or the SMS gateway times out. It's not a specific diagnostic message; the app gives you zero hints about which of these problems you're facing.

This error also pops up when OfferUp's database already has your number attached to another account, or when the number is a known VoIP line. Free texting app numbers (TextNow, Google Voice, etc.) are almost always burned at this stage because OfferUp recognizes the carrier fingerprint.

Here's what to do:

  1. Check whether the code arrived. If it didn't, the SMS gateway failed; skip to step 4.

  2. Check if the code expired. OfferUp codes last roughly 10 minutes. If yours is older, request a fresh one.

  3. Clear the app's cache. On Android: Settings → Apps → OfferUp → Storage → Clear Cache. On iOS, offload the app, then reinstall it.

  4. Wait 15 minutes and retry. This resets the rate limiter on OfferUp's side.

  5. Try a different number. If the error persists, OfferUp has flagged your number in its system; no amount of retrying will fix that.

The error rarely indicates a typo in the code itself; it's usually a server-side mismatch between what OfferUp sent and what their database expects. Also note that if you've swapped SIMs recently, your phone may still report the old number to OfferUp's servers; check your device settings first.

OfferUp Verification Code Invalid: Causes and Fixes for Expired or Wrong Codes

An "invalid code" error almost always means you took too long to enter it, or the SMS gateway delivered a different code than the one on OfferUp's screen. Codes expire in roughly 10 minutes, and if you've requested a resend, the old code is instantly killed even if it's still visible in your messages app.

The fix is to request a fresh code, make sure your screen timeout doesn't lock the app, and input the code manually instead of relying on autofill.

Here's why autofill betrays you:

  • Stale code insertion: Google Messages or iOS Passwords autofill can insert a code from a previous request. Clear the field and type manually.

  • Timestamp check: Look at the SMS timestamp; if it's older than 10 minutes, don't bother entering it.

  • Leading zeros get stripped: Autofill sometimes drops leading zeros. OfferUp's code is always six digits; if you got eight, you're looking at a different service's message.

  • Number recycling: If you're using a temporary number and getting "invalid," the number may have been recycled from a previous user who already burned it on OfferUp. Request a new number entirely.

OfferUp Unable to Verify Phone: Network, Carrier, and App-Level Issues

When OfferUp says it's "unable to verify" your phone, the problem is usually on the network side: your carrier isn't routing the SMS, or your device is stuck on a weak signal that can't deliver short codes. App-level bugs also cause this, especially if you haven't updated OfferUp in months or your OS is out of date.

Check your cellular signal strength, switch from Wi-Fi to data, and update both the app and your operating system before troubleshooting further. If the issue is carrier-specific, other SMS verifications (like WhatsApp's) will also fail, which narrows the problem to your network, not OfferUp.

Additional troubleshooting:

  • Toggle Airplane Mode for 30 seconds. This forces your phone to re-register with the carrier network and can fix SMS delivery.

  • Check international routing: If you're abroad, short-code messages may be blocked entirely by international SMS routing. You'll need a local number or a US-based temporary number.

  • Update everything: OfferUp updates frequently break or fix verification. The latest version matters more than you think.

  • Test another SMS service: Try verifying with WhatsApp or Telegram using the same number. If that also fails, it's a carrier issue.

  • Rooted or jailbroken devices: These sometimes trigger anti-fraud checks that block verification features. If that's the cause, there's no fix; use a different device.

OfferUp Phone Verification Error: The "Unknown" Problem and How to Avoid It Safely

The "Unknown" verification error on OfferUp is one of the most frustrating issues because it offers zero context; the app fails to confirm your number without explaining why. It's typically caused by a carrier lookup timeout, a number with a mismatched SIM, or OfferUp's fraud detection blocking your IP alongside your number.

A safe avoid is to switch from Wi-Fi to cellular data before retrying, since VPNs and carrier-grade NATs confuse OfferUp's verification system. If that fails, wait 24 hours and try again on a completely different network.

Here's what triggers the "Unknown" error most often:

  • VPN connections: This is the #1 trigger. OfferUp flags mismatched IP and carrier geolocation when your VPN routes through a different country than your SIM.

  • Carrier lookup timeout: Your carrier's database is slow to respond, and OfferUp's servers give up waiting.

  • Previously banned number: A number used on a suspended OfferUp account inherits the ban flag, causing the "Unknown" error.

  • Country mismatch: A number from a different country than your IP address triggers this error instantly.

  • Filtered or blocked SMS: Some carriers block short-code messages from marketplace apps entirely. The FCC regulates SMS spam, but carrier filtering policies vary widely; you can check the FCC's consumer guide on unwanted messages for context on how carriers handle short-code blocking.

If you've ruled all of these out and the error persists, a dedicated temporary number designed for verification services is your cleanest path forward.

OfferUp Verification SMS Not Received: Carrier Filtering, Spam Folders, and Timing

If OfferUp's verification SMS never arrives, your carrier is the first suspect. Short-code messages from marketplace apps are frequently caught by spam filters that silently drop them. Check your messaging app's spam or blocked folder first, and look for a notification saying "Messages from unknown senders are filtered."

Timing also matters. Verification gateways are slower at peak hours (evenings and weekends), and if you've requested several codes in a row, your number may be throttled for 24 hours. Moving to a different network, using an alternate SIM, or switching to a fresh temporary number resets that throttle.

Here's your ordered fix list:

  1. Check spam/blocked folders in your messaging app. iOS "Filter Unknown Senders" and Android's spam protection both hide verification codes.

  2. Disable carrier scam blocking. T-Mobile's Scam Block and similar features on other carriers can be toggled off temporarily for the verification window.

  3. Check for "Message Blocked" alerts. If your phone shows this, your carrier has hard-blocked the short code; call support and ask them to unblock it.

  4. Switch from Wi-Fi to cellular data. Some routers block short-code delivery entirely.

  5. Wait 24 hours if you've hit the resend cap. After five failed attempts, OfferUp locks the number for a day. Waiting is the only fix.

  6. Try a different number. A fresh, SIM-backed temporary number resets the throttle and avoids your carrier's short-code filters.

For international users, carrier filtering intensity varies by country. US carriers are among the most aggressive at filtering short codes. A UK number for receiving SMS or an Indian number may behave differently depending on your region.

Once the code lands and you clear the verification screen, your account shifts to "verified" status, but the process isn't done. Immediately add a backup login method (Google or Apple sign-in) and adjust your notification preferences so OfferUp stops sending promotional SMS to your confirmed number.

If you used a temporary number, screenshot the verification confirmation and save your login credentials. You may need to re-verify later, so keep proof of your verified status if you need to contact OfferUp support.

Here's your post-verification checklist:

  • Check for the "Verified" badge on your profile. If it's missing, restart the app and force-sync your account.

  • Delete the verification SMS, especially if you're on a pay-per-use temporary number.

  • Set a 30-day calendar reminder to check whether your account requires reverification, especially if you used a rental number. Some users report needing to reverify after policy changes or listing reports.

  • Link a payment method now so future "verified seller" promotions activate automatically.

  • Confirm your listing limits. Verified accounts can post more free listings per week than unverified ones; knowing your cap helps you plan.

Verified accounts also unlock higher trust signals with buyers, which matters in a marketplace where scammers are a genuine concern. The SMS step is the gateway to full functionality; treat it as the beginning, not the end.
