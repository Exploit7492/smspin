What happened to SMS-Activate

There was no long wind-down. The service stopped, and a short notice replaced it — which is still the only authoritative account of the closure. Everything below is quoted from that notice rather than from second-hand reports, several of which circulate the wrong date.

“SMS-Activate service is closed. We have completely ceased our operation since December 22. Thanks to everyone who has been with us for these 10 years.”

— the closure notice published on sms-activate.org
  1. 22 Dec 2025

    Operations ceased

    SMS-Activate stopped issuing numbers. Its homepage now reads simply “SMS-Activate service is closed”, thanking users “for these 10 years”. Activations and API calls stopped resolving the same day.

  2. 5 Feb 2026

    Withdrawals closed

    The window to withdraw a remaining balance ended. Users were notified by email and via the Telegram bot; after this date the notice states plainly that “there is no one who can deal with your requests”.

  3. 30 Apr 2026

    Support window ended

    The final contact address for urgent questions stopped being monitored. There is now no official channel for balance recovery or account access.

  4. Today

    Only clones remain

    The domain still resolves, but it serves the closure notice — nothing else. Any site presenting a working SMS-Activate login is not the original service.

SMSPin is not affiliated with SMS-Activate and had no involvement in its closure. We cannot access, restore, or refund SMS-Activate accounts or balances.

!

Any “SMS-Activate” still online is a clone

This is not our characterisation — it is what the operators wrote on the way out:

“Our name goes away with us. If you find a service called SMS-Activate, then be sure they are scammers.”

Several sites now run on lookalike domains, some reproducing the original login page. A working SMS-Activate sign-in form in 2026 is the clearest possible signal that a site is not the original service. Do not enter old credentials — if you reused that password anywhere, change it — and do not deposit funds. This applies to every replacement you consider, including ours: check the domain before you pay.

What to check in a replacement

A feature-by-feature table against a service that no longer runs would be theatre. These are the four things that actually decide what a replacement costs you — worth asking of any provider on your shortlist, not only this one.

Where the numbers actually come from

Strict apps — WhatsApp, Telegram, Google — quietly drop SMS to number ranges they distrust. Ask whether a service delivers over real carrier SIMs on in-country devices, and whether it will tell you the delivery rate for the route you are buying, not a sitewide average.

What happens when no code arrives

This is the single biggest cost difference between services. SMSPin refunds your balance automatically the moment an activation window closes without a code — no ticket, no waiting. You pay for codes that arrive, not for attempts.

Whether you can get your money out

The shutdown stranded balances because funds sat on the platform. Top up in small amounts, keep no more float than a few days of usage, and prefer a service with a published refund and payment policy you can read before you deposit.

How much rewriting the API costs you

Tens of thousands of integrations broke at once because they were pinned to one vendor’s query-string API. A clean, documented JSON API is worth an afternoon of migration — and makes the next migration, whenever it comes, far cheaper.

SMS-Activate shutdown — FAQ

What happened to SMS-Activate?

It closed. According to the notice on its own homepage, SMS-Activate “completely ceased operation since December 22” (2025), after about ten years. The shutdown was a decision by its operators rather than a technical failure, and the site now serves only that farewell notice.

Can I still withdraw my SMS-Activate balance?

No. The notice states withdrawals were available until 5 February 2026 and that users were notified by email and in the Telegram bot. After that date it says explicitly that there is no longer anyone able to process requests, and the support address stopped being monitored on 30 April 2026. If you still hold a balance, treat it as unrecoverable.

I found a site called SMS-Activate that still works — is it real?

No. The operators addressed this directly in their closing notice: “Our name goes away with us. If you find a service called SMS-Activate, then be sure they are scammers.” Several clones are live on lookalike domains, and some reproduce the original login page. Do not enter credentials or send funds to any of them.

Is SMSPin the official SMS-Activate successor?

No, and we will not claim to be. SMSPin is an independent service with its own operator network, pricing and API. We are not affiliated with SMS-Activate, we did not acquire it, and we cannot access, restore or refund SMS-Activate accounts or balances. This page exists because people searching for a replacement deserve accurate information about what happened.

Will my existing SMS-Activate scripts work if I change the URL?

No — and any service telling you it is a one-line change is overselling it. SMS-Activate used a query-string API (handler_api.php with an action parameter); SMSPin uses a JSON REST API. Requesting a number becomes POST /api/v1/orders and polling becomes GET /api/v1/orders/{id}. The good news is that the short service codes are the same (wa, tg, and so on), so only the transport and the country format change. Most integrations are a short afternoon of work.

How do country codes differ?

SMS-Activate identified countries by numeric id (0 for Russia, and so on). SMSPin uses standard ISO-2 country codes — RU, ID, GB, US. You can pull the full list your key can order from at GET /api/v1/countries, and the service catalogue at GET /api/v1/services, rather than hard-coding a mapping table.

What happens if a code never arrives?

Each activation holds the number for 20 minutes. If no code arrives in that window, the order expires and the charge is refunded to your balance automatically — you do not need to open a ticket. That is the mechanism that makes the effective cost per delivered code lower than the sticker price suggests.

What payment methods does SMSPin accept?

Crypto through Cryptomus (USDT, BTC, ETH, BNB, TRX and 20+ others) and cards and regional methods through Payssion. Minimum top-up is $5. There is no PayPal and no Stripe.

Is using a virtual number for verification legal?

Receiving an SMS verification code on a virtual number is legal in most countries. You remain responsible for following the terms of service of the platform you are verifying on and the law where you live. SMSPin is not affiliated with SMS-Activate or with any app you verify against.
