If you're seeing the "Only valid numbers are allowed" error when trying to sign up for Uber, here's the truth: your number might be perfectly functional and still get rejected. Uber doesn't just check that your number looks right; it runs a carrier-level lookup that flags VoIP, Google Voice, and many virtual numbers as invalid for driver accounts.

This guide is for prospective Uber and Uber Eats drivers who have hit this frustrating wall. It explains exactly why Uber rejects certain numbers, walks you through the fixes that actually work, and shows you how to get a verified number without burning your personal SIM.

#Why Uber Says Invalid Phone Number on the Driver App

Uber's driver app doesn't just check that your number looks correct; it runs a carrier-level lookup to detect VoIP, Google Voice, and many virtual number services. When that lookup returns a non-mobile or unregistered status, Uber blocks it with the generic invalid phone number message. This is Uber's fraud prevention layer, not a typo issue.

The message appears both during signup and when you change your number in the driver dashboard. Uber uses a third-party carrier lookup that flags numbers not tied to a physical SIM card. Landlines, Google Voice, and most free texting apps are automatically rejected.

The error can persist even if the number is technically active, because Uber checks the underlying carrier type. If your number is valid but still rejected, the carrier database likely hasn't updated your number's port status yet.

#7 Real Reasons Uber Rejects Your Phone Number

Uber can reject your phone number for several reasons, including unsupported number types, previous use, or formatting issues. The exact reason may vary by country, carrier, and Uber's current verification requirements. If your number is rejected, check these common possibilities:

  • VoIP number: Uber may not accept numbers from internet-based calling services for verification.

  • Landline: Some verification flows require a mobile number that can receive SMS.

  • Previously used number: A number linked to another account may cause a verification issue. Contact Uber support if you believe the number was incorrectly restricted.

  • Unsupported region: Uber may not offer the same verification options in every country.

  • Free virtual number: Publicly shared numbers may be unsupported or already in use.

  • Recently ported number: Carrier information may take time to update after you switch providers.

  • Recycled number: A reassigned number may still be associated with an existing account or previous activity.

If your number falls into one of these categories, repeatedly retyping it may not solve the problem. First, verify the country code and format, confirm that the number can receive SMS, and check whether Uber supports its number type.

#How Uber Validates Phone Numbers Before You Can Drive

Uber runs a multi-step validation: formatting check, country code match, carrier database lookup, and a live SMS delivery attempt. The carrier lookup is where most valid numbers fail, because Uber specifically filters for numbers assigned to major mobile carriers in your region. Numbers that pass all four checks get the OTP; anything else gets the invalid error.

The formatting check verifies country code, area code, and digit count match your declared region. The carrier lookup queries HLR Home Location Register databases to confirm the number is a real mobile line. This is the same technology Twilio and other telecom providers use for number intelligence; it tells you the carrier, line type, and whether the number is active.

Uber's validation is stricter for driver accounts than rider accounts because of insurance and fraud requirements. Changing your number mid-account triggers a fresh validation, not a cached check.

#Bypass Only Valid Numbers Are Allowed on Uber

The fix is simple in concept: you need a real mobile number that Uber recognizes as tied to a physical SIM. If you don't have one, the only workaround is a premium virtual number service that sources real, SIM-based numbers from legit carriers. Free apps and VoIP numbers won't pass.

Here's the exact sequence to get past the error:

  • Confirm your country code matches your Uber account region exactly. A mismatch triggers an instant rejection.

  • Test the number on a fresh browser session in incognito mode to rule out cookies or cached data.

  • If using a virtual number, verify it's a real SIM number, not a VoIP line. Check the service's FAQ or feature list.

  • Request the OTP up to 3 times, waiting 60 seconds between attempts. Uber rate-limits after that.

  • If it still fails, switch to a different number entirely. Don't retry the same rejected number; you'll burn time.

#Uber Valid Phone Number Not Working? Fixes That Actually Work

When your number is genuinely valid, but Uber still rejects it, the carrier lookup database is usually out of sync, especially if you recently ported your number from one carrier to another. The fix is to force a re-validation by removing the number completely, logging out, and clearing the app cache before re-adding it.

  • If you ported a number recently, wait 24–48 hours for the carrier database to update before trying again.

  • Remove the number from your Uber account entirely, then re-add it after a full logout.

  • Use Uber's website, not the mobile app, for re-validation; it sometimes uses a different lookup path.

  • Check that your number isn't on a do-not-route list for premium SMS services. Some carriers block short-code messages by default.

  • Contact Uber Support as a last resort; mention a carrier lookup mismatch, not just invalid, to get a faster resolution.

If you're in a hurry and your SIM is flagged, a paid carrier-backed number is the fastest alternative.

#Temporary Number for Uber Verification: What Works and What Doesn't

Free temporary number websites rarely work for Uber because Uber bans known number pools. Paid virtual number services that offer real SIM-based lines can work, but only if the service explicitly supports Uber verification and uses fresh numbers not flagged in spam databases. The key difference is whether the service routes through an actual carrier or just a VoIP gateway.

What doesn't work:

  • Free SMS-receive sites with shared numbers, already burned in Uber's system.

  • VoIP-based numbers from any provider, paid or free.

  • Numbers recycled from other platforms, for example, WhatsApp or Telegram verification pools.

What can work:

  • Paid services with private or dedicated real-SIM numbers.

  • Services that specifically list Uber as a supported platform, meaning they've tested their carrier routes.

  • Services that offer an automatic refund if the OTP never arrives, which signals reliable infrastructure.

#Uber Eats Driver Valid Phone Number: Same Rules, Separate App

Uber Eats driver accounts use the same phone-validation backend as Uber rides, so if you get an invalid error in the Eats app, it's the same carrier-lookup issue. The difference is that Eats drivers are often part-time and might be tempted to use a secondary number. Still, Uber treats both apps as one ecosystem, so if one rejects a number, the other will reject it immediately.

Your Uber Eats and Uber rides accounts are linked; a number change on one syncs to the other. The only valid numbers are allowed error appears on the Eats app with identical wording. Eats delivery partners signing up offshore, where US SIMs are hard to get, are the highest-demand segment for real-SIM virtual numbers.

If your Eats app works but your rides app doesn't, check that you don't have two separate accounts with conflicting numbers. A number that passes Uber Eats validation will always pass Uber rides validation, and vice versa.

#Alternate Number for Uber OTP: When and How to Use One

An alternate number helps when your primary number is temporarily unavailable, but VoIP or free virtual lines can cause problems. The right approach is to use a paid service that rents a real mobile number tied to an actual carrier in a supported country, then redeem the OTP and complete the verification before the window expires.

Alternate numbers are best for the initial OTP verification, not for ongoing account changes. When Uber sends an OTP, it's only valid for 10 minutes; your service must poll for the code in real time. If you're using an alternate number, avoid closing the Uber app or navigating away from the OTP screen.

The alternate number's country code must match your account region exactly. After verification, you can switch back to your primary number without re-triggering a full validation in most cases.

#Keep Your Uber Account Secure: Do's and Don'ts for Number Changes

Once you've verified with a valid number, resist the urge to switch to a free number later; it will trigger an immediate re-validation and likely lock your account. Uber's fraud system monitors numbers that change frequently or jump between carrier types, so each change increases the risk of manual review or an account hold.

Do's:

  • Verify with a real SIM number first, then switch to a different real SIM number if needed.

  • Log out and log back in after any number change to force a fresh session token.

  • Complete a trip or delivery within 24 hours of changing your number to signal active, legitimate use.

Don'ts:

  • Don't use a Google Voice number at any point; even after your account is active, it can flag you retroactively.

  • Don't save multiple numbers to the same account; Uber flags accounts with multiple recent number changes.

  • Don't use a temporary number to bypass a suspended account; that violates Uber's terms and can result in permanent deactivation.

#FAQ

Is it legal to use a temporary or virtual number for Uber driver verification?

Using a temporary or virtual number for legitimate verification can be legal, but you must follow Uber's current Terms of Service and applicable local regulations. Make sure the number belongs to you or is legitimately provided for your use, and don't use it to misrepresent your identity or bypass Uber's verification requirements. SMSPin is not affiliated with Uber or any other app or website.

Why do OTP codes sometimes fail to arrive even with a valid number?

A valid number does not guarantee SMS delivery. Carrier filtering, SMS routing issues, number-type restrictions, recycled numbers, or temporary rate limits after repeated requests can prevent a code from arriving. Check the number and country settings, wait if you've made several requests, and use the latest code when it arrives.

What's the difference between a one-time verification number and a rental number?

A one-time verification number is generally intended for a single SMS verification and may become unavailable afterward. A rental number stays active longer and can receive multiple messages during the rental period. If you legitimately need continued access to the same number, a rental may be a better fit.

What should I absolutely NOT use a temporary phone number for?

Don't use temporary numbers for banking, government services, financial account recovery, or other situations that require reliable long-term identity verification. You should also never use a temporary number to bypass a suspended Uber account, evade security controls, create fraudulent accounts, or misrepresent your identity.

My Uber driver phone number is invalid even though it's my real SIM. What do I do?

First, check the country selection and number format, then make sure your SIM is active and can receive SMS. If you recently changed carriers or ported the number, the carrier information may not be fully updated yet. Restart the app, sign out and back in if appropriate, and try again later. If the error continues, contact Uber Support rather than assuming the number itself is defective.

Can Uber deactivate my account for using a virtual number?

Uber may take action when a number or account activity violates its policies or triggers security checks. Uber doesn't automatically accept virtual numbers, and using a paid service doesn't remove that risk. Follow Uber's current requirements and be prepared to verify your identity or account information if requested.

How do I know if my intended number will work before paying?

Look for a provider that clearly states which countries and number types it supports and explains its refund or replacement policy for failed activations. No provider can guarantee that Uber will accept a particular number because acceptance depends on Uber's verification system. A clear refund policy can reduce the risk of paying for an activation that doesn't receive an OTP.
